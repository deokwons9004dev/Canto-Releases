3. WHAT CAN LEAVE YOUR MAC, AND ONLY WHEN YOU CHOOSE

Some features send data off your Mac when you use or turn them on. Settings ›
Memory & privacy › What leaves this Mac lists each one, with a switch for every
one that can send by itself; when a switch is off, Canto does not send that
data.

  • Web search: when a chat or research looks something up, the words of the
    search go to the search services you set up, and pages, including links you
    paste into a chat, are read from their sites (or through a search service
    you set up, when a site turns Canto away).
  • Site icons: with Site icons on, the icon of a web source is fetched from
    that source’s own site when it is shown.
  • Pictures from the web: a picture in a note or chat that points at a website
    loads from that site when it is shown, so the site sees that your Mac opened
    it; no note text is sent. With it off, such a picture loads only when you
    press Load on it.
  • Pages you link to: with it on, Canto fetches the web pages, videos and PDFs
    your notes link to, from their sites.
  • Cloud models: off until you connect a cloud provider (such as OpenRouter) or
    add a server that is not on your Mac, and you can turn them off in What
    leaves this Mac. When you send a chat to a cloud model, your question, the
    chat so far and the notes that chat reads go to that provider and to the
    company that runs the model, under their terms. Unless you change it, Canto
    asks OpenRouter to use only hosts that keep nothing. Canto never sends notes
    to a cloud model for work you did not start, such as meetings, titles, tags
    or search. You pay the provider directly; we never receive your notes, your
    questions or your key.
  • Sync: with sync on, your notes go to your own iCloud account, encrypted on
    your Mac first with a key only your Macs have. Apple cannot read them and we
    never receive them.
  • Other apps (MCP): with Canto’s MCP server on, apps you connect can read your
    notes, and with Pro change them. What they do with your notes is up to those
    apps.
  • Publishing: a note you publish goes to LonelyDuck’s publishing service when
    you press Publish, and is public at its address until you unpublish it.
  • License checks: Canto Free sends nothing about a license. With Pro, your Mac
    activates once and then renews with LonelyDuck about twice a week, sending
    your license, an identifier for your Mac made for that license, and your
    Mac’s model, never your notes. Canto also reads an anonymous list of
    switched-off licenses, so a refunded or released license stops working; you
    can turn these live updates off.
  • Update checks: at launch and every 6 hours Canto asks GitHub, where its
    releases are published, whether there is a newer version. You can turn this
    off and check by hand.
  • Usage data: off unless you turn it on. It sends LonelyDuck counts of which
    features are used and anonymous errors, never the text of your notes.
  • Model downloads: models come from Hugging Face when you choose to download
    one.
  • Diagnostics: a report is sent only when you press Send diagnostics, after
    you have seen everything in it.

macOS itself may download speech or Apple Intelligence models from Apple when
you use those features.
